Chapter 1Getting started

Tideline Journal is a private place to write each day. Everything you write is encrypted with your password, and nothing leaves your computer.

Creating your journal

The first time you start the program it asks you to choose a password. Type it twice and press Create password.

  • A password must be at least 8 characters. A short sentence you will remember makes a good password.
  • Straight after creating a journal you are offered a recovery key. Make one (see chapter 12): it is the only way back in if you ever forget the password.
Important

Your journal is encrypted for real. If you forget the password and have no recovery key, nobody can open it, including the people who made this program. Make a recovery key and keep it somewhere safe.

Unlocking and locking

Each time the program starts it shows an unlock screen. Type your password and press Enter. Below the password box are two links: Forgot your password? (use a recovery key) and Open a different diary… (use a journal stored in another folder, such as a backup).

To lock the journal at any time, press Ctrl + L or choose Lock journal at the bottom of the side menu. Locking forgets the decrypted text. If you were in the middle of writing an entry, it is kept in memory and is still there after you unlock.

The journal also locks by itself after a period without activity, and hides itself while the window is in the background (see chapter 12).

Your window at a glance

  1. Title bar. The search box (Ctrl + F) is in the middle and the window buttons are on the right. Drag the bar to move the window; double-click it to maximise or restore.
  2. Side menu. New entry, places to go (Today, Calendar, Timeline, Pinned, Photos, Insights), your categories with a count of entries, then Lock journal and Settings.
  3. The calendar. Arrows, the month title, Today and Surprise me, and the Year / Month / Week / Day switch.
  4. The day panel. A summary of the day you have selected, with its entries as cards.
Tip

You can make everything bigger or smaller with Ctrl + + and Ctrl + − (and Ctrl + 0 to reset). F11 switches full screen on and off.

Chapter 2The calendar

The calendar shows what you have written and when. Click any day to select it and see its entries on the right.

Month view

This is the view you start in. Each day is a tile:

  • The date number is at the top left. Today sits in an amber circle, and the selected day has a teal outline. Days belonging to the previous or next month are dimmed.
  • A small coloured dot at the top right shows the day’s average mood, from red (Awful) through amber to teal (Great).
  • Each entry appears as a chip: a dot in its category colour, the time and the title. A star on a chip means it is pinned. If a day has more entries than fit, the tile says “+2 more”: click the day to see them all on the right.

Year, Week and Day views

Use the switch under the month title (or Ctrl + 1 to Ctrl + 4).

Year

A heat-map with one small square per day: the more you wrote, the brighter the square. Hover for the date and word count, click to select a day, double-click to open it in Day view. Underneath are your totals for the year (entries, days written, words, current and longest writing streak, average mood), entries per category and a chart of your mood through the year.

Week

Seven tall columns, one per day, with more room for each entry’s chip.

Day

The selected day hour by hour. Entries sit in the hour they were written.

Moving around

Dragging entries

To move an entry to another day, press on its chip and drag it onto the day you want; a teal outline shows where it will land. The time of day is kept. In Day view you can drag an entry to another hour to change its time. Press Esc to cancel a drag.

Starting an entry from the calendar

Double-click a day to write a new entry for it, or double-click an entry’s chip to edit that entry. In Day view, double-clicking an hour starts an entry at that time.

Chapter 3The day panel

The panel on the right is your reading view for the selected day.

The top of the panel

  • The weekday and date, then a summary such as “2 entries · 75 words · Mood: Okay”.
  • Write about this day starts a new entry for the selected day.
  • Summarise appears when the optional AI helpers are switched on and the day has entries (see chapter 14).
  • If you set a daily word goal (Settings → Writing), a thin progress bar shows how close the day is.

Entry cards

Every entry is a card showing its category, the time, a star (click it to pin or unpin), the title, a few lines of text and the word count.

  • Open opens the entry in the writing view so you can read it in full or change it.
  • The ⋯ button has Delete and, for a private entry that is showing, Hide again.
  • Show more appears when an entry is longer than the preview, has several paragraphs or contains a picture (it then reads “Show more, with picture”). Click it to read the whole entry inside the card, pictures included; click Show less to fold it up again.
  • Attached files are listed on the card with Save as… and Open.

Deleting and undoing

Deleting an entry moves it to Recently deleted (see chapter 13). A message appears at the top of the panel with an Undo button for the one you just removed.

Private entries

A private entry is shown as grey placeholder lines and “Private entry – click to show”. Click it to read it. It hides itself again when you lock the journal or leave the window.

Days with no entries

An empty day shows a dashed box that says “A blank page.”

On this day

Underneath the cards, “On this day” lists what you wrote on the same date in earlier years. Click one to jump to it.

Chapter 4Writing an entry

Writing has a whole page to itself, so there is nothing in the way.

Opening the writing page

There are several ways in: New entry in the side menu, Write about this day, Ctrl + N, double-clicking a day, or Open on an existing entry. A new entry belongs to the selected day and takes the current time.

At the top, ← Calendar takes you back, Discard throws the entry away, and Save entry saves it. “Unsaved draft” tells you there are changes that are not saved yet.

Date, time and category

The three chips at the top left set when the entry belongs and what it is about.

  • Date: click the chip to open a small calendar and pick any day.
  • Time: click the chip, then use the hour and minute boxes or press Now.
  • Category: click the chip and choose from your categories.

On the right, Pin stars the entry so it appears under Pinned, and Private hides it until you click it (see chapter 12). Both are switches: click again to turn them off.

Mood

Under “How are you feeling?” choose one of five moods: Awful, Low, Okay, Good or Great. The chosen mood glows in its colour. Click it again to clear it. Moods feed the dots on the calendar and the charts in Insights, but they are entirely optional.

Writing prompts

The green card offers a question to get you started, such as “What do you want more of in your life?”.

  • Use this prompt puts the question into your entry as a heading at the cursor, on a line of its own, and leaves you ready to write the answer. Use it again, anywhere in the entry, for as many prompts as you like.
  • Another shows a different question without adding anything.
  • The × hides prompts. You can switch them back on under Settings → Writing → Writing prompts.

The card stays visible while you write, so you can pick a new prompt halfway through an entry.

The writing card

Type an optional title at the top (press Enter to move into the text), then write below it. What you see is what you get: bold looks bold, headings are large, lists have bullets. Underneath, a line shows the word count and an estimated reading time.

Formatting marks

The little symbols behind formatting (such as the stars around bold text) are hidden so the page looks clean. The arrow keys, Backspace and Delete step over them as if they were not there. If you want to see them, tick Show formatting marks at the bottom of the card.

Pictures

Press the picture button and choose an image, or simply drag an image file onto the window. It appears in the text where your cursor was. A Remove picture button sits under each one. Large images are shrunk to a sensible size (1,600 pixels on the longest side) and stored inside your encrypted journal. Because pictures are part of the journal file, many large pictures make it bigger and saving slower.

Attachments

Press the paperclip to attach any file, up to 25 MB each, or drag a file onto the window. Attached files are listed under the card with a Remove button, and are stored inside the encrypted journal. On a day’s card you can Save as… a copy or Open it.

Good to know

Open makes a temporary plain copy so another program can show it. The copy is kept in a folder beside your journal (opened-attachments-temporary) and removed when you lock the journal or next start the program.

For your safety

Programs and scripts (such as .exe, .bat, .ps1, .vbs, .msi and .lnk files) can be attached and saved as a copy, but they are never opened from the journal, so a file in a journal someone else gave you cannot start a program on your PC. Such a file shows “can’t be opened here” instead of an Open button.

Templates

Press Template and choose one to fill the page with headings and questions to answer. Manage templates… opens the templates window (see chapter 10).

Ask AI

Ask AI opens a menu. If the helpers are not set up, it offers Set up AI helpers…. Once they are on, it offers to turn your rough notes into an entry and to suggest a question to think about (see chapter 14).

Saving, discarding and word goals

  • Save with Save entry, Ctrl + S or Ctrl + Enter. The journal is encrypted and written to disk each time.
  • Discard or ← Calendar leaves without saving. If you have written something, the first click turns the button into “Click again to discard”, so you can’t lose work by accident; clicking anywhere else cancels it.
  • With a daily word goal set, “Today so far” under the card shows progress for the whole day, counting this entry and the day’s other entries.
  • Ctrl + D pins or unpins the entry while you write.

Changing or deleting an entry later

Press Open on its card, or double-click its chip. You can change anything, including its date, time, category, mood and pictures. To delete it, use ⋯ → Delete on its card.

Chapter 5Finding things

Search the whole journal in a moment, or browse everything newest first.

Search

Click the search box at the top (or press Ctrl + F) and start typing. The day panel is replaced by the results as you type, and matching words are highlighted. Click a result to jump to its day.

You typeIt finds
river walkEntries containing both words, in any order and any capitals.
"river walk"Entries with that exact phrase.
river -workEntries with “river” but not “work”.

Search also looks at category names, dates (such as 2026-10) and attachment file names. Private entries are never found by their text; they can only turn up by category or date.

Filters

Press Filters in the search box, or just start with the filters and no words at all. You can narrow results by:

  • Category and Mood,
  • Time: any time, the last 7 or 30 days, the last year, or “Between dates…”,
  • Starred only and Has attachments,
  • and sort by Newest first, Oldest first or Best match.

Clear (top right of the results) empties the search and the filters and returns to the day view.

Timeline

Timeline in the side menu lists every entry, newest first, in the same results panel. It is the quickest way to scroll back through everything.

Categories in the side menu

Each category in the side menu shows how many entries it holds. Click one to see only that category’s entries. Edit next to “Categories” opens the category editor (see chapter 9).

Chapter 6Pinned

Star the entries you want to be able to find again in a second.

Pin an entry by clicking the star on its card, using the Pin switch while writing, or pressing Ctrl + D in the writing page. Pinned entries carry a star on the calendar. Pinned in the side menu opens them all on one page.

  • Each card shows a date tile, the day and time, the category, the mood, the title and a short preview with the word count.
  • Show more opens the whole entry in the card, pictures included.
  • Open day takes you to that day on the calendar.
  • Click a card’s star to unpin it.
  • The chips at the top (All, then one per category) filter the cards, and Sort by orders them by newest, oldest or longest.
  • The dashed card at the end reminds you how to pin more.

Chapter 7Photos

Every picture you have added, in one place.

Photos in the side menu shows all the pictures in your journal as thumbnails, newest first, grouped by month.

  • Drag the Thumbnail size slider to make the pictures smaller or bigger.
  • Click a picture to see it large. The arrow keys move between pictures, and from the viewer you can jump to the entry the picture belongs to.
  • Pictures from private entries are marked, and the entry they belong to stays hidden until you reveal it.

Chapter 8Insights

Patterns in how you feel and how you write, worked out from your own entries and shown only to you.

Choose Insights in the side menu. At the top right, switch the range between 30 days, 90 days, This year and All time. There are three tabs.

Mood patterns

  • Average mood and Entries with a mood sum up the range.
  • The green card shows how many ratings you need before the charts are reliable. Once you have ten, it lists plain-language observations instead, such as which time of day you tend to feel best.
  • The bar charts show your average mood by day of the week, time of day, length of entry and category. The scale along the bottom runs from 1 (awful) to 5 (great). A dimmer bar means fewer than three entries, so it is only an early signal.
  • View as table (below the charts) shows the same numbers as a table.
Remember

These are patterns in your own ratings, not causes. A quiet bar may simply mean you rarely wrote at that time.

Writing habits

Totals for entries, words and words per entry; the words you use most (bigger means more often; everyday filler words and private entries are left out); charts of when you write by hour of the day and day of the week; and your longest entries (click one to jump to it).

Look back

A recap of a week, month or year: choose This week, Last week, This month, Last month, This year or Last year at the top right. You get your totals, how your mood compared with the period before, your happiest and hardest days, where you wrote, words that kept coming up, pictures from the period, and entries to remember. Save this review as a PDF… at the bottom makes a printable copy.

Chapter 9Categories

Categories are the coloured labels that say what an entry is about.

Open the editor with Edit next to “Categories” in the side menu.

  • Rename: click a name, type the new one and press Enter. Every entry using that category is updated.
  • Change the colour: click the coloured square next to a name and pick a colour.
  • Add: type a name under “Add a category”, choose a colour and press Add.
  • Delete: press Delete next to a name. The category goes, but its entries are kept and shown in grey.

Chapter 10Templates

A template fills a new entry with headings and questions, so you never start from a blank page unless you want to.

Open the templates window from Settings → Writing → Templates → Manage, or from the Template menu in the writing page.

  • Eight built-in templates: Gratitude, Daily review, Evening wind-down, Weekly reflection, Mood check-in, Goals check-in, Dream log and Travel log.
  • Built-in templates cannot be changed. Press Make my own copy to get an editable version.
  • New template starts one from scratch. Give it a name and write its text. You can use {date}, {weekday} and {time}, which are replaced automatically when the template is used.
  • Used for every new entry lets you choose a template that is filled in each time you start a new entry (or “None”).

Chapter 12Privacy and security

Your journal is encrypted on disk and never sent anywhere. These are the tools that keep it that way.

Your password

Your entries, pictures and files are sealed with strong encryption (XChaCha20-Poly1305) under a key that is itself protected by your password (Argon2id). Without the password, the file is unreadable noise. To change it, use Settings → Security → Password → Change password, type the new password twice and press Change password.

The recovery key

A recovery key is a long code, shown once, that can open your journal if you forget your password. Open Settings → Security → Recovery key.

  1. Press Create a recovery key. The key appears once on the screen.
  2. Press Copy or Save as text file… and keep it somewhere safe and away from this PC: written down, or in a password manager.
  3. Tick “I have stored my recovery key somewhere safe”, then press Done.

Later the same window lets you make a new key (the old one stops working) or remove the key. Each of these needs a second click to confirm.

If you forget your password

  1. On the unlock screen choose Forgot your password?
  2. Type your recovery key and choose a new password.
  3. The journal opens, with the new password from now on.
Important

Anyone who has both your recovery key and your journal file can open your journal, so keep the key private. If you have no recovery key and forget your password, the journal cannot be recovered by anyone.

Private entries

Switch on Private while writing and the entry is hidden until you click it. While hidden, its text, mood, pictures and search matches are not shown. It hides again when you lock the journal or leave the window. Private entries are also left out of searches by text, Surprise me, Insights, PDF exports (unless you ask for them) and the AI helpers.

Auto-lock and hiding in the background

Auto-lock locks the journal after a period of no activity (10 minutes unless you change it). Hide when in the background blanks the whole window as soon as you switch to another program or minimise it, so no one can read it over your shoulder when you step away. Click the window to show it again.

What is and isn’t protected

  • The journal file and its automatic backups are encrypted.
  • PDF exports are not encrypted. They are a plain copy of what you chose, so keep them somewhere safe.
  • While the journal is unlocked, the decrypted text is in the computer’s memory. Lock it when you step away.
  • Nothing is sent over the internet. The optional AI helpers talk only to a program running on your own PC.

Chapter 13Your data

Where your journal lives, how to back it up and how to get things back.

Where your journal is stored

By default your journal is in a folder called data beside the program. Inside it are entries.json (your encrypted journal), backups (automatic copies) and config.json (a few settings that are needed before you unlock). The folder is easy to find and copy.

Open Settings → Your data → Data location → Change… to see exactly where it is.

  • Show in Explorer opens the folder.
  • Move diary to another folder… copies your journal to a folder you choose and uses it from there.
  • Use a diary from another folder… opens a journal that already exists elsewhere, such as a backup. You will be asked for its password.

Automatic backups

The first time you open the journal each day, and only if it has changed, a dated copy of the encrypted file is saved in the backups folder. They are encrypted exactly like your journal, so they are safe to keep and to copy elsewhere. Choose how many to keep under Settings → Your data (the default is 10).

Settings → Your data → Backups and restore → Open… shows the list.

  • Back up now makes a copy straight away; Open backups folder shows the files.
  • Delete removes one backup.
  • Restore… puts a backup back in place. Click it a second time to confirm. Your current journal is first saved as a “before restore” safety copy, then the journal locks and you unlock it with the password that backup had when it was made (which may be an older password).
Tip

Automatic backups live next to the journal, so they won’t help if the whole drive is lost. Use Back up… now and then to save a copy to a different drive.

Export to PDF

Settings → Your data → Export to PDF → Export… or Ctrl + E makes a printable PDF.

  • Choose what to include: this day, this week, this month, this year or everything (each shows how many entries it holds).
  • Tick or untick Include pictures.
  • Private entries are left out unless you tick Include private entries.
  • Press Create PDF… and choose where to save it.
Careful

A PDF export is not encrypted or password protected. Keep it somewhere safe, and delete it when you no longer need it.

Recently deleted

Deleting an entry moves it here for 30 days, then it is removed for good. Open it from Settings → Your data → Recently deleted → Open….

  • Restore puts an entry back on its day (with its pictures).
  • Delete for good removes one permanently.
  • Empty the bin removes everything. Click it a second time to confirm.

Taking your journal with you (portable use)

The program is a single file, tideline-journal.exe, and needs no installer. Copy it, together with the data folder, onto a USB drive and run it from there on any Windows PC: your journal stays on the drive.

  • Use “Safely remove” before unplugging the drive.
  • Files you open from entries are copied into the data folder (not onto the PC you are visiting) and removed when you lock.
  • The program is not code-signed, so Windows may show “Windows protected your PC”. Choose More info → Run anyway. A PC with Smart App Control switched on may refuse unsigned programs entirely.

Chapter 14AI helpers

Three optional helpers that run entirely on your own computer. They are off until you set them up.

What they do

  • Turn my notes into an entry (in the writing page’s Ask AI menu): jot rough notes, then have them written up in your voice. You choose Add below my notes or Replace my notes (or Write it again).
  • Summarise (on a day’s panel): writes a short summary of that day’s entries and can save it with Save as an entry, marked “AI summary”.
  • Suggest a question to reflect on (Ask AI menu): proposes a thoughtful question based on your recent entries, which you can use with Use as a heading (or ask for Another question).

Setting them up

Open Settings → Writing → AI helpers → Set up. The window walks you through three steps.

  1. Ollama. The helpers use Ollama, a free program that runs AI models on your PC. If it is not installed, the window offers to install it for you (you tick a box confirming that you understand it downloads a large file), or to open its download page. If it is installed but not running, Start Ollama launches it, and Check again re-checks after you install.
  2. A model. Pick one that is installed, or download one (for example Llama 3.2, Qwen 2.5, Gemma 2 or Phi-3 mini). Bigger models write better but are slower and need more memory. A progress bar shows the download.
  3. Switch the helpers on. Tick Use the AI helpers. You can try the model first with Test it. The helper buttons then appear.

How your privacy is protected

  • The program only ever talks to Ollama on this PC. Nothing is sent over the internet.
  • Private entries are never sent, and neither are summaries the AI wrote earlier.
  • The helpers are told to use only what you wrote and not to invent anything. Each result window has Show exactly what was sent to Ollama.
  • Nothing is saved until you choose to, and you can edit the text first. Entries containing AI text are marked AI-assisted; day summaries are marked AI summary, and don’t count towards your word counts, streaks or statistics.
Good to know

The first answer can take a minute while the model loads into memory. The helpers are entirely optional; the rest of the program works the same without them.

Chapter 16Troubleshooting and questions

I forgot my password

If you made a recovery key, use Forgot your password? on the unlock screen (see chapter 12). Without a recovery key there is no way back in, which is what keeps your journal private. If you have an older backup whose password you remember, Open a different diary… can open it.

The window is hiding my entries

“Hide when in the background” blanks the window while it is not the one you are using. Click the window to show it again, or turn the setting off under Settings → Security.

The journal locked while I was away

That is Auto-lock. Enter your password to continue; anything you were writing is still there. You can lengthen or turn off Auto-lock under Settings → Security.

I deleted an entry by mistake

Press Undo at the top of the day panel straight away, or open Settings → Your data → Recently deleted and press Restore. Entries stay there for 30 days.

I can’t find an entry

Try Timeline or search with fewer words. Check that no filter is switched on in the search box, and remember private entries can’t be found by their text. They show on their day as “Private entry”.

Saving fails, or the program says it cannot find a path

The journal folder may be on a drive that is full, read-only or no longer plugged in. Check the drive, or use Settings → Your data → Data location to see where the journal is meant to be.

A restored backup asks for a different password

A backup keeps the password it had when it was made. Use that password (or its recovery key).

Windows warns that the program may not be safe

The program is not code-signed. In the “Windows protected your PC” window choose More info → Run anyway. If Windows refuses outright because Smart App Control is on, you can use the program on a PC where that is switched off.

Letters or symbols appear as empty boxes in a PDF

PDF exports use the fonts installed on your PC. Characters those fonts don’t have, such as some emoji, show as boxes.
